## RESEARCH (Step 3 — prior art)
- **Item writing.** The multiple-choice item-writing literature treats a distractor that is also a defensible key as a defect that ruins the item (Haladyna, Downing & Rodriguez, 2002, a review of item-writing guidelines). A named trap is our distractor; the same rule applies.
- **The five unbounded traps.** Booth Sweeney & Sterman (2000) and Cronin, Gonzalez & Sterman (2009) document that people expect a stock with a constant inflow to grow without limit even when its outflow rises with the stock. The trap is real and central to the construct. Encoding it needs a reply format for "no limit", which is a scoring decision.
- **Build vs reuse:** build. The bench already has an audit that fails on unearned claims; the trap guard is the same idea applied to traps.
- Appended to `SystemsBenchResearch.MD` (append-only).

## THOUGHT PROCESS (the narrative)
The instruments on the company's home page said the practice was overdue, so the right move was the practice, and the run read the disk before choosing. The tempting lever was CoT truncation, the open half of the faithfulness axis; it sits higher on the ladder, but its value arrives with the first live run. INV-004 corrupts every run now, dry or live: a model that reads "which day had the biggest drop" instead of integrating the flows gets full marks on an item built to catch exactly that. It went unnoticed because the trap was never encoded, and it was never encoded because it could not be told apart from the answer. A missing value hid a broken item. That is why the lever is a guard and not only a fix: the next item with the same flaw now fails the audit instead of falling quietly out of the trap rate.
The five unbounded traps are the honest edge of this run. They are central to the construct (people believe a stock with a constant inflow grows forever), and the bench cannot yet record a reply that says so. Deciding how is a scoring rule, so it waits for the council, and the first live run's replies should inform it.
